How much gravel do I need?

Multiply length by width by depth to find volume. Because depth is entered in inches, convert it to feet first, then divide cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards.

Cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 324

Worked example: 40 × 12 ft driveway at 4 inches

A 40-foot-long, 12-foot-wide driveway at a compacted depth of 4 inches contains 160 cubic feet, or 5.93 cubic yards, before extra material.

The 6.5-yard order is an example based on a supplier selling half-yard increments. Ask the actual supplier about order increments, density, moisture, compaction, and whether its quoted yardage is loose or compacted.

Converting gravel yards to tons

Gravel is commonly sold by the ton, while project volume is measured in cubic yards. A broad planning estimate is 1.4 tons per cubic yard, but the actual weight changes with stone size, material, and moisture. Use the density quoted by your landscape supplier whenever possible.

Should I add extra gravel?

Yes. A 5–10% allowance covers low spots, compaction, and small measurement errors. Irregular areas and deep bases may need more. This calculator includes an adjustable overage in the result.
